The set list

Four ways to get worked on. Tell us what your body is asking for when you sit down — we'll read the room.

Chair massage

Clothes on, shoes on, twenty minutes and you're back in the crowd. Neck, shoulders, and the knot the camping pad gave you.

Table massage

The classic. Draped, warm blankets, deep or gentle as you like it, under prayer flags with the lake right there.

Thai on the mats

Assisted stretching on mats in the grass, fully clothed. Big opening for hips and hamstrings after a weekend of dancing.

Ashiatsu, barefoot

Bodyweight pressure delivered by feet, with overhead bars for control. Broad, deep, and strangely gentle. Ask for it by name.

Four hands. Sometimes six.

Two or three of us working the same body at once, in tandem — it is the closest thing we have to playing a chord. Your brain stops tracking whose hands are where and simply gives up the fight.

The band

Three therapists, three different pairs of hands. Book by name, or let us match you to whoever fits what your body is doing today.

Thai massage

Nalani S.

Nalani works on the mats, fully clothed, moving you through assisted stretches that reach places a table can't. Hips, hamstrings, shoulders, breath. If you've spent three days dancing in a field, start here.

Best for: stiffness, long drives, dancers, anyone who'd rather stay dressed.

Myofascial release

Alice S.

Alice works slow. Myofascial release is sustained, patient pressure that waits for tissue to let go instead of forcing it — less like kneading, more like a long conversation. Old injuries and stubborn holding patterns are her territory.

Best for: chronic tightness, scar tissue, people who say massage never sticks.

Ashiatsu & chair work

Seraphim S.

Seraphim works barefoot from the overhead bars, using bodyweight instead of thumbs — broad, deep, and far kinder than it sounds. She also runs the chair for the quick twenty-minute resets between sets.

Best for: big backs, deep pressure seekers, short sessions on a tight schedule.

Playing together

Two of us on one table is a 4-handed massage. All three is six hands. Same session length, more hands moving at once — book it as an add-on when you pick your slot, subject to who's free that hour.
